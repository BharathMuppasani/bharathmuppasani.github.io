What the two cuts show together

  • Realistic applications and learned policies barely overlap. Construction, assembly, inspection and field operations are almost entirely non-learning — classical scheduling and optimisation. The learned policies cluster in warehouse/tool-delivery, search & rescue, object transport and abstract allocation.
  • The largest single application is not an application. Abstract allocation and scheduling instances dominate, which is why so many of these methods have never met perception, grasping or a physics engine.
  • Method follows heterogeneity type. Trait-vector papers reach for attention/graph networks and hypernetworks, because capabilities are an input vector. Morphologically heterogeneous papers reach for macro-actions or LLMs, because the gap between an arm and a quadrotor is easier to bridge with discrete skills than with a shared action space.
  • Information-gathering is an application here, not a decision. In the inspection and mapping row, coverage is the goal. No paper in this corpus treats inspection as one option competing against acting directly — which is the gap the project targets.
  • LLM methods buy embodiment diversity cheaply. The most physically varied teams (quadrotor + quadruped + arm) are all LLM-coordinated, because prompting sidesteps the problem of a shared learned representation across bodies — at the cost of optimising nothing.
