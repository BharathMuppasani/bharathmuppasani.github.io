Classical Limits

Classical Planning Assumptions Break as Soon as the World Changes During Execution

The warehouse example was easy to model because we assumed a known, static, deterministic world. Real systems violate those assumptions constantly.

Unpredictable events

A hallway can close after the plan is already computed.

Non-deterministic actions

The same move command may not always finish in the same place or time.

Partial observability

The robot may not know a blockage exists until it reaches the corridor.

Continuous changes

Traffic, humans, and timing constraints evolve while the plan is being executed.

Moving obstacles

New constraints appear from outside the planner's original model.

Imperfect execution

Sensors drift, objects slip, and real action effects can deviate from the model.

A plan can be valid at time t and invalid at time t+1. That is the central motivation for dynamic planning methods.
