Lecture Map

Outline

One running warehouse robot example will connect graph search, planning models, optimization objectives, and learning-based control.

Part I

Why planning?

Sequential decisions, real deployments, and why optimizing over trajectories differs from one-shot optimization.

Part II

Search on graphs

BFS, DFS, and A* on the same warehouse maze, with the heuristic intuition made explicit.

Part III

Modeling for planning

Robot world, PDDL, symbolic states, actions, and search over implicit planning states.

Part IV

Optimization and heuristics

Constraint optimization, metric-sensitive optimality, heuristic guidance, and planner search control.

Part V

Dynamics

When classical assumptions fail, how replanning responds, and where fixed plans stop being enough.

Part VI

Learning

Why learned policies become necessary, and how supervised signals differ from reward-based RL.

Main claim: planning is best understood as search and optimization over state transitions, with heuristics and learning added when scale and dynamics demand them.
