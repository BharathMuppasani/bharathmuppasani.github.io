Bridge to Learning

Why Replanning Alone Is Not Enough When the Model Is Incomplete or Costly to Engineer

Planning works well when the transition model, action costs, and state representation are known. Learning becomes necessary when those pieces are uncertain, missing, or constantly changing.

When planning is enough

Known model, known objective, clear state.

If the warehouse map is reliable, travel costs are accurate, and goals are explicit, a planner can search efficiently and replan when disruptions are observed.

hand-coded actions known costs explicit goal facts
When learning is needed

Unknown costs, missing dynamics, noisy observations.

Now the bottleneck is not search alone. We may need to learn how congestion affects travel time, how sensors imply blockage states, or how to act well under recurring uncertainty.

unknown travel-time model sensor-to-state inference repeated uncertain decisions
Warehouse example 1

Predict corridor blockage or delay.

A planner can use the prediction, but it may not know how to produce it from raw sensor data without learning.

Warehouse example 2

Choose robust delivery behavior under recurring uncertainty.

If the same disruptions happen over many episodes, learning reusable behavior becomes attractive.
